Numerical study on the influence of input power and tube shape on a single-phase natural circulation for passive residual heat removal heat exchanger
Introduction
Natural Circulation Loop (NCL) has been used in many Generation III reactors to passively remove the decay heat from the reactor core under accident conditions (Schulz, 2006). It takes advantage of natural forces or phenomena (such as the gravity, buoyancy and natural heat convection) to remove the decay heat in time without pumps, diesel engines, other active equipment or external power sources. Therefore, natural circulation with the advantage of simple structure improves the inherent safety of the system. An NCL consists of a heater at the lower elevation, a cooler at the higher elevation and a pipe connecting them. The heat and cooler can be placed either along the vertical arm or the horizontal arm. Four different configurations can be obtained according to the locations of the heater and cooler: (1) vertical heater and vertical cooler (VHVC); (2) vertical heater and horizontal cooler (VHHC); (3) horizontal heater and vertical cooler (HHVC); (4) horizontal heater and horizontal cooler (HHHC). Fig. 1 shows a simplified HHHC with possible cooler inlet and outlet. The natural circulation takes different fluid densities as the driving force to complete a natural cycle. The direction of the flow is affected by the locations of the heater and the cooler. When the fluid is warmed by the heater, the temperature increases and the density decreases, creating a driving force that causes it to flow upward. Then the fluid enters the cooler, the density increases and the corresponding driving force causes the fluid to flow downward to be warmed by the heater. The cycle repeats until the driving force is not sufficient to move the fluid.
The flow inside the NCL may move forward and backward and cause issues of instabilities. An unstable NCL may lead to problems in control and occurrence of Critical Heat Flux (Swapnalee and Vijayan, 2011; Vijayan et al., 2019). Many researchers have conducted theoretical, experimental and numerical studies to gain insights into factors affecting the oscillatory behavior of the NCL. Damerell and Schoenhals (1979) investigated the effect of the angular displacement of the heater and cooler in a toroidal thermosiphon experimentally and theoretically. Chen's study (1985) shows that the aspect ratio (height/width) plays an important role in the stability of an NCL. The calculated neutral stability conditions show that the flow is least stable when the aspect ratio of the loop approaches unity. Misale et al. (2000) studied the influence of the wall thermal capacity and axial conduction over a single-phase NCL using a 2-D numerical code. Vijayan et al. (2007) experimentally studied the effect of heater/cooler orientations and heating power on instabilities in rectangular single-phase NCL's. Their study suggests that an HHHC results in an unstable loop while others result in a stable loop. Vijayan et al. (2008) studied the effect of the loop diameter on the stability of a rectangular loop both theoretically and experimentally. The instability threshold was found to decrease with a larger loop diameter in single-phase loops. Devia and Misale (2012) studied thermohydraulic behavior of a single-phase HHHC. They found that after a flow reversal, the temperature difference was initially small but immediately grew up until the next flow reversal. Naveen et al. (2014) proposed and validated a friction factor correlation for single-phase NCL dynamics with various power inputs. Kudariyawar et al. (2016) studied the influences of different heater/cooler orientations and heating power on instabilities in rectangular single-phase NCLs using Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) simulations. Researchers have found that flow dynamics in HHHC configuration is the most complex (Pilkhwal et al., 2007; Vijayan et al., 2007; Kudariyawar et al., 2016), and many studies have been conducted to analyze the influence of the heating power on flow behavior in an HHHC configuration. Pilkhwal et al. (2007) compared the capability of one-dimensional models and CFD models to predict dynamic phenomena in a single-phase NCL at different power inputs. They concluded that CFD was effective in showing the origin of pulsating instabilities. Naveen et al. (2011) used 1-D computation to predict unidirectional oscillations, bidirectional oscillations, and chaotic switching with different heating power inputs. Basu et al. (2013) applied a 2% perturbation in the mass flow rate and found that the system reached a neutrally-stable condition for an input power of 1670 W. They concluded that any power level below this limit would result in a stable system, and any input power level above the limit would lead to an unstable system with oscillation growth. Misale (2016) conducted experiments to study influences of different power inputs on the behaviors of a single-phase NCL and noted that the frequency and amplitude of the oscillation increased as the power increased. Misale et al. (2021) conducted experiments to investigate the influence of mutual interaction between two adjacent single-phase NCL's with different diameters of the loops and heating rates. However, it is noted that little attention has been paid to the case with the cooler in an NCL being partially horizonal and partially vertical, such as the passive residual heat removal heat exchanger (PRHR HX) in a nuclear power plant as shown in Fig. 2. In the NCL, water is heated in the reactor vessel and cooled via the C-shaped heat exchanger tubes. In this study, effects of different power input on oscillation and heat transfer performance of the PRHR HX under single-phase natural circulation condition are investigated using CFD, which could properly predict temperature oscillations (Devia and Misale, 2012). In addition, effects of the shape of the heat exchanger tube-C-shaped tubes and spiral-shaped tubes (Wu et al., 2022)-are discussed.

Conclusions
Flow behavior in an NCL is important to performance of the passive safety system in a nuclear power plant. The influences of power inputs and the configuration of the heat exchanger tube on flow behavior and heat transfer performance under single-phase natural circulation conditions are analyzed numerically in this study.
